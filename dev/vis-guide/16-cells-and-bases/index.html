    Cell types and bases¶

    Every cell, every space and every basis Tack supports today, in one place, and what is planned. Function Space Fundamentals explains what a space and a basis are, and Fields and Spaces how to make and convert fields in them.

    Cell shapes¶

    A topology's cells are shapes from tack.dataset.shapes. Most have a reference element, a fixed parametric cell that every cell of the shape is a map of; bases are defined on it. Tack's shapes, their corner orders and their parametric coordinates are VTK's classic linear cells'.

    Shape Dimension Corners Reference element VTK type
    Vertex 0 1 a point VTK_VERTEX (1)
    Line (segment) 1 2 [0, 1] VTK_LINE (3)
    Triangle 2 3 the unit triangle VTK_TRIANGLE (5)
    Quad 2 4 [0, 1]², corners counterclockwise VTK_QUAD (9)
    Pixel 2 4 [0, 1]², corners x fastest (an axis-aligned quad) VTK_PIXEL (8)
    Tetra 3 4 the unit tetrahedron VTK_TETRA (10)
    Hexahedron 3 8 [0, 1]³ VTK_HEXAHEDRON (12)
    Voxel 3 8 [0, 1]³, corners x fastest VTK_VOXEL (11)
    Wedge 3 6 the unit triangle times [0, 1] VTK_WEDGE (13)
    Pyramid 3 5 [0, 1]³ with its top collapsed to the apex VTK_PYRAMID (14)
    PolyLine 1 any none VTK_POLY_LINE (4)
    Polygon 2 any none VTK_POLYGON (7)
    Polyhedron 3 any none VTK_POLYHEDRON (42)

    The topologies that hold them:

    Topology Its cells
    StructuredTopology a grid's lines, quads or hexahedra, numbered by index (images, rectilinear and curvilinear grids)
    UnstructuredTopology any mix of the shapes with a reference element, of any dimensions, in one topology
    PolylineTopology polylines (lines are two-point polylines)
    PolygonalTopology polygons (triangles and quads are polygons too)
    PolyhedralTopology polyhedra, each a list of faces

    Polylines, polygons and polyhedra have no reference element. A field on them is linear on pieces: on a polyline's segments, on the triangles that fan a polygon from its points' mean, and on the sub-tetrahedra that fan a polyhedron's faces from their means and the cell's. So linear fields are exact there, and integrate, probe, gradients and the cutting filters all work on these pieces.

    Spaces¶

    Space Values Basis
    H1(data, order=p) shared between the cells around each node a continuous polynomial of order p in each cell
    L2(data, order=p) each cell's own a polynomial of order p in each cell, discontinuous between cells
    Constant(data) one per cell none: the value everywhere in the cell
    Values(data, on) one per point, cell, face or edge none: data attached to entities
    SideTraces(data) per face, per side, per face corner two-sided face values

    What each shape supports¶

    yes: supported; —: refused, with a message saying why.

    Shape H1 order 1 H1 order 2 H1 order 3 and up L2 order 0 L2 order 1 L2 order 2 and up Curved geometry
    Vertex yes — — yes yes — —
    Line yes yes yes yes yes yes yes
    Triangle yes yes yes yes yes yes yes
    Quad, Pixel yes yes yes yes yes yes yes
    Tetra yes yes yes yes yes yes yes
    Hexahedron, Voxel yes yes yes yes yes yes yes
    Wedge yes yes yes yes yes yes yes
    Pyramid yes — — yes yes — —
    PolyLine, Polygon, Polyhedron yes (linear on pieces) — — Constant — — —

    There is no fixed upper order (the tests go to order 6). An L2 order may also vary per cell (orders 0 and 1, L2(data, order=array)).

    A curved geometry is a geometry field above order 1, in H1, or in L2 for cells that do not meet (a mesh displaced by a DG field, a periodic mesh). Cells are then located, probed, measured, integrated, cut and drawn as they are, not as their corners make them. A field's order may differ from the geometry's: its nodes are placed where the curved geometry puts them, bit for bit the same in every piece.

    Bases¶

    A space above order 1 names its basis, the family of functions its values are the coefficients of:

    Basis What its values are Spaces Shapes
    "gauss_lobatto" values at Gauss–Lobatto nodes (Lagrange); MFEM's default H1, L2 lines, quads, pixels, hexahedra, voxels
    "equispaced" values at equispaced nodes (Lagrange); VTK's Lagrange cells and gmsh's H1, L2 every shape with a basis
    "gauss_legendre" values at Gauss–Legendre nodes, inside the cell (Lagrange); MFEM's default L2 L2, from order 1 lines, quads, pixels, hexahedra, voxels
    "bernstein" Bernstein coefficients (VTK's Bezier cells, MFEM's positive bases) H1, L2, from order 2 every shape with a basis
    "serendipity" order 2 without face centres and interiors: quad8, hex20, wedge15 (the full space on triangles and tetrahedra) H1, order 2 triangles, quads, pixels, tetrahedra, hexahedra, voxels, wedges

    When no basis is given above order 2, it is equispaced where the mesh has triangles, tetrahedra or wedges, which have no other nodes, and Gauss–Lobatto otherwise. At order 2 and below, Gauss–Lobatto and equispaced nodes are the same.

    Two layouts and one numbering go with a space:

    • L2's layout: a tensor basis' values are stored either each cell's together (layout="blocks", MFEM's and VTK cell grids' layout) or cell-fastest (layout="cells", faster on GPUs from order 3). "auto" chooses by device.
    • Element-to-DOF tables: H1(data, order=p, dofs=table) numbers values by a row per cell, as MFEM and many solvers do, instead of by entity.

    Mixing shapes¶

    An UnstructuredTopology may mix every shape with a reference element, and every space above applies to the mix: a mesh of hexahedra and wedges holds one H1 field of order 3. The filters that cut cells into linear sub-cells (subdivide, contour, slice, clip, threshold) take any mix of one dimension. Each family is refined alone, and the parts are joined where they share lattice points. On such a mix, crossings are placed on the linear sub-cells; exact crossings need cells of one family.

    Reading and writing other formats¶

    VTK unstructured grids. Linear cells read as the shapes above. Higher-order cells read as H1 fields of the grid's largest order, in any mix of shapes, dimensions and orders:

    VTK cells Read as
    Lagrange (68-73: curve, triangle, quadrilateral, tetrahedron, hexahedron, wedge) equispaced H1 of their order
    Bezier (75-80) H1(basis="bernstein")
    quadratic edge, triangle, tetrahedron (21, 22, 24) H1 of order 2
    quadratic quad, hexahedron, wedge (23, 25, 26: quad8, hex20, wedge15) serendipity, or the full order-2 space beside other cells
    biquadratic quad, triquadratic hexahedron, biquadratic-quadratic wedge (28, 29, 32) H1 of order 2
    quadratic-linear quad and wedge, biquadratic-quadratic hexahedron (30, 31, 33) H1 of order 2 (exact: their spaces lie in it)
    biquadratic triangle (34), cubic line (35) H1 of order 3 (exact)
    quadratic, triquadratic, Lagrange and Bezier pyramids (27, 37, 74, 81), quadratic polygons (36) refused

    dataset_to_vtk(..., higher_order=...) writes Lagrange, Bezier or VTK's classic quadratic cells.

    VTK cell grids (vtkCellGrid, .dg files). These need a VTK newer than 9.7.1.

    Cell grid basis Read as
    HGRAD C1, shared H1 order 1 (copied)
    HGRAD C1, per cell L2 order 1 (copied)
    constant Constant
    HGRAD C2, I2, A (any order), G (Gauss points), Bezier resampled by VTK onto Tack's equispaced space of that order: H1 shared, L2 per cell (exact)
    HCURL, HDIV, and pyramids' bases other than C1 left out, with a warning

    dataset_to_vtk(data, cell_grid=True) writes C1 at order 1 and VTK's A basis above it.

    MFEM. mfem_to_dataset reads H1 and L2 collections of any order (Gauss–Lobatto, equispaced, Gauss–Legendre and positive bases), MFEM's older collections, nonconforming (AMR) meshes, periodic meshes (an L2 geometry), and curved meshes. mfem_grid_function writes back.

    See the Data Model reference for each function.

    Planned¶

    For 0.8:

    • Higher-order pyramids: VTK's quadratic (13 nodes), triquadratic (19), Lagrange and Bezier pyramids, and cell grids' C2 and pyramid19. Their bases are rational, so they come with:
    • Rational Bezier cells: VTK's Bezier cells with weights other than 1.
    • H(curl) and H(div) at order 1, with Piola maps, on every shape but the pyramid. These are cell grids' HCURL and HDIV, now left out.
    • More bases natively:
    • the Gauss-point bases on triangles, tetrahedra and wedges (now resampled into equispaced ones);
    • serendipity per cell (L2);
    • the enriched F2 cells (15-node tetrahedron, 21-node wedge);
    • modal bases;
    • an order per axis, as VTK's quadratic-linear cells have (now embedded in the full order).
    • Adaptive subdivision for the cutting filters, per cell from the cut's error.

    Not planned: higher-order polygons and polyhedra (VTK's quadratic polygon among them).
