    Physical Units¶

    A field's values are numbers; what they measure is its unit. Tack keeps units on fields, not arrays: an array is storage and a field is meaning, so kernels, devices and implicit arrays never see a unit. A unit is a string such as "m/s", "K" or "Msun/kpc**3", kept in unyt's canonical spelling, so "m/s", "m*s**-1" and "m/s^1" are one unit. None is unknown, which is not the same as "dimensionless": nothing derived from an unknown unit is guessed.

    The spatial unit is the geometry field's (data.unit), and the dataset's origin is in it.

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

# A raster at State Plane coordinates, in US survey feet, with a temperature.
image = td.uniform_grid((5, 4, 3), spacing=(10.0, 10.0, 10.0), unit="ftUS")
image.origin = (1_400_000.0, 600_000.0, 0.0)
celsius = np.linspace(0.0, 30.0, image.num_points).astype(np.float32)
image.fields["T"] = td.Field(td.H1(image), tack.array(celsius), unit="degC")
print(image.unit, image.fields["T"].unit)

# Filters that keep a quantity keep its unit, and the spatial unit with it.
cut = td.clip(image, "T", 15.0)
print(cut.unit, cut.fields["T"].unit)
assert cut.unit == "ftUS" and cut.fields["T"].unit == "°C"

# convert scales on the device; the origin converts in double precision.
metres = td.convert(image, "m")
kelvin = td.convert(metres, "K", field="T")
assert isinstance(metres.geometry.values, tack.arrays.UniformCoordinates)  # still an image
assert np.allclose(metres.world_positions(), image.world_positions() * 1200 / 3937)
assert np.allclose(kelvin.fields["T"].values.numpy(), celsius + 273.15)

# Derived quantities derive their units; a difference of degrees Celsius is in
# kelvin, so a temperature gradient is in kelvin per foot.
assert td.gradients(image, "T").unit == "K/ftUS"
stats = td.statistics(image, "T")
print(stats.unit_of("mean"), stats.unit_of("population_variance"))
assert stats.unit_of("population_variance") == "K**2"

# Parameters take quantities: a threshold in degrees Celsius on a field in
# kelvin, a radius in metres on positions in feet.
warm = td.threshold(kelvin, "T", "20 degC", "30 degC")
assert warm.num_cells == td.threshold(kelvin, "T", 293.15, 303.15).num_cells
cloud = td.point_cloud(image.world_positions(), unit="ftUS")
coarse = td.voxel_downsample(cloud, "10 m")             # 32.8 US survey feet
assert coarse.num_points == td.voxel_downsample(cloud, 10 * 3937 / 1200).num_points

# Positions in two different units do not meet.
try:
    td.probe(image, td.point_cloud(np.array([[1_400_010.0, 600_010.0, 5.0]]), unit="m"))
except ValueError as error:
    print("refused:", error)

    Where units come from¶

    • Field(space, values, unit=); DataSet(..., unit=), uniform_grid, rectilinear_grid and point_cloud take unit= for the geometry, and point_cloud(..., units={name: unit}) for its fields. A geometry Field brings its own unit.
    • OctreeDataSet(..., units={name: unit}) (and from_cells): octree fields are arrays, so their units are a mapping; the spatial unit is the roots'.
    • The VTK interop reads and writes each array's UNITS_LABEL, the one unit VTK 9.7.1 keeps: on point and cell data, the points' array, a rectilinear grid's axes and a HyperTreeGrid's arrays and axes. VTK has nowhere to keep an image's spatial unit. A label that is no unit (VTK's labels are free text) is dropped with a warning; ^ is read as a power, as LLNL units and VTK write it.

    Code units are scaled units, "3.0857e21*cm", which need no registry of their own. Besides unyt's units, Tack knows the US survey foot, ftUS (1200/3937 m): State Plane coordinates and older LiDAR use it, and it differs from the international foot by 2 parts per million, metres at survey coordinates.

    What keeps a unit¶

    Filters carry fields through carry, so every field a filter takes along keeps its unit: clip, slice, threshold, extracts, contour (with contour_value in the contoured field's unit), tetrahedralize, shrink, warp, transform, probe, pieces, ghost cells, merging and to_device. So do the filters whose result is the same quantity: project, values_at_centers, traces, magnitude, voxel_downsample's means, SPH interpolation, and cell_centers and face_centers (positions, in the spatial unit).

    Cylindrical and spherical coordinates give an unknown spatial unit, since an angle and lengths are no one unit.

    Derived units¶

    Filters that compute a new quantity derive its unit from their inputs':

    Filter Unit
    gradients the field's per spatial unit; flow_quantities' divergence and vorticity the gradient's, the Q-criterion its square
    jump the field's
    dot, cross the product of the two, by its SI name where it has one (N times m is J)
    composite the fields' one unit (two known units are refused)
    log_values a pure number, of a pure number (a dimensioned field is refused)
    cell_geometry measures in the spatial unit to the cells' dimension (m**3, m**2, m), centroids in it
    face_geometry, edge_lengths areas in the spatial unit to the faces' dimension (an edge's length on a surface), unit normals pure numbers; lengths in it
    net_flux, divergence, perot the flux's; it over the cells' measure; the faces' components'
    statistics Statistics.unit and weight_unit, and unit_of(name) for each number: the mean, extremes and deviations in the field's unit, the variances in its square, sum times the weights', skewness and kurtosis pure numbers
    deposit counts pure numbers, sums, means and extremes in the field's; per_volume over the cells' measure (1/m**3, kg/m**3)
    sph_density, sph_interpolate the mass per volume; the field's, times m / (rho L^d) (a pure number for consistent units)
    halo_catalog, outliers, point_normals masses in the mass field's, radii and centres and mean distances in the spatial unit; normals pure numbers
    advect, streamlines time in the spatial unit over the velocity's
    interpolate(..., unit=) what the caller says: a function's code does not say its unit

    A difference of values with an offset zero -- degrees Celsius or Fahrenheit -- is in the absolute unit of the same size, kelvin or Rankine: gradients, jumps, variances and deviations. Such values are not multiplied (dot refuses them): convert to kelvin first. Labels, ids, flags and counts that are not quantities (region, cell_ids, outlier, neighbors) have no unit, and a result computed from a bare number -- upwind_flux's constant velocity, a mass given as one number, the potential's G -- is unknown.

    Quantities as parameters¶

    A parameter in a unit takes a quantity: a string such as "10 cm", a pair (value, unit) (the value a number or an array), or a unyt or pint quantity. It is converted to what the parameter is compared with; a plain number is taken to be in that unit already, as before.

    Parameter Converted to
    voxel_downsample's size; radius= of outliers, connected_components, point_normals, ghost_points; periodic= boxes; PointLocator's bin size and neighbors' radius; SPH smoothing lengths given as one number; the halo catalogue's softening; slice_plane's origin and distances the spatial unit
    threshold's and threshold_points' bounds, contour's values, clip's value by a field the field's unit, offsets included ("20 degC" on a field in kelvin)
    advect's and streamlines' step size the spatial unit over the velocity's
    a mass or density given as one number to SPH or the halo catalogue, upwind_flux's velocity none: the quantity brings its unit, so the result has one (mass=(1e10, "Msun") makes densities in Msun/kpc**3)

    A quantity needs a unit to convert to: one given for a dataset or field of unknown unit is refused, as is one for an implicit function's values, which have none. A string that names a field (smoothing_length="h") is the field.

    Mismatches are refused¶

    Positions compared or moved between datasets must be in one spatial unit, as they must be relative to one origin: probe's points, pieces of a PartitionedDataSet, the boxes of an AMRDataSet and an octree's probe refuse two known units, naming both and convert. An unknown unit cannot be checked, so it is not refused.

    Converting¶

    convert(data, unit) gives the dataset with its geometry in unit, and convert(data, unit, field=name) with that field in it: a new dataset, the values scaled (and offset, from degrees Celsius to kelvin, say) on the device, everything else kept. The geometry's conversion scales the origin on the host in double precision, so survey coordinates keep their digits; an image stays an image and a rectilinear grid keeps its axes. The unit converted from must be known. Integer fields convert only by relabelling ("1000*m" to "km").
