    Reduce: statistics and regions¶

    Some answers are a few numbers, not a new mesh: the range and mean of a field, how its values spread, how many separate regions a threshold leaves and how big each is. Tack's reductions compute them. statistics and entropy summarize a field, as VTK's vtkDescriptiveStatistics does, and a summary can be weighted by volume or mass. connected_components labels the connected regions of a mesh or a point cloud, as vtkConnectivityFilter does.

    Summarize a field¶

    statistics(data, field) returns a Statistics of a scalar field's values: their count n, min, max, sum and mean, the central moments, the sample and population variances and standard deviations, the skewness and the kurtosis. It computes them as Viskores' Statistics filter does, with the moments taken about the mean in a second pass.

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

data = td.uniform_grid((21, 21, 21), origin=(-1, -1, -1), spacing=(0.1, 0.1, 0.1))
r = np.linalg.norm(data.positions(), axis=1)
data.fields["r"] = td.Field(td.H1(data), tack.array(r))

s = td.statistics(data, "r")
print(f"n {s.n}, range {s.min:.3f} to {s.max:.3f}, mean {s.mean:.4f}, "
      f"std {s.sample_stddev:.4f}, skewness {s.skewness:.3f}")
# n 9261, range 0.000 to 1.732, mean 1.0077, std 0.2909, skewness -0.364
assert s.n == data.num_points
assert np.isclose(s.mean, r.mean(dtype=np.float64), rtol=1e-6)
assert np.isclose(s.sample_variance, r.astype(np.float64).var(ddof=1), rtol=1e-5)

    The numbers are Python floats, so the call waits for the device work it reads. Their definitions:

    Name Is
    m2, m3, m4 the sums of (x - mean)^k
    sample_variance, population_variance m2 / (n - 1) and m2 / n
    sample_stddev, population_stddev their square roots
    skewness sqrt(n) m3 / m2^1.5, 0 for a constant field
    kurtosis n m4 / m2^2, not the excess kurtosis, and 0 for a constant field
    unit the field's unit, which mean, min and the others are in

    Integer fields are summarized in floating point.

    Ghosts are left out. Cells and points that the dataset's ghost marks mark as copies owned by another piece, or as hidden, are not counted. A PartitionedDataSet is summarized whole: field names a field every piece has, each piece reduces what it owns on its own device, and the parts are combined in the pieces' order over every MPI rank. The result is the same however the pieces are shared out among ranks. statistics also takes AMR datasets, counting each region of space once at its finest level, and octrees, counting their leaves (adaptive data).

    See the reference for the full signature, and Statistics for every field it holds.

    Weight by volume or mass¶

    statistics summarizes the stored values, each counted once. On a mesh whose cells differ in size, that is not the mean over the volume: a region of many small cells counts for more than a region of a few large ones. weights weighs each value:

    • weights="volume" weighs each cell by its measure. The field must be cell data (a Constant field), whose values belong to the cells. Point data is refused.
    • weights= a field's name weighs each value by that field, on the same entities. A density gives mass-weighted moments.

    With weights, mean is sum(w x) / W, sum is sum(w x), and weight is W, the sum of the weights. The example's grid is fine near x = 0 and coarse toward x = 8:

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

axis = np.concatenate([np.linspace(0, 1, 11), [2.0, 4.0, 8.0]])   # graded along x
data = td.rectilinear_grid(axis, [0.0, 1.0], [0.0, 1.0], unit="m")
data.fields["T"] = td.Field(td.H1(data), tack.array(data.positions()[:, 0].copy()), unit="K")
data.fields["T_cell"] = td.project(data, "T", td.Constant(data))   # each cell's average

samples = td.statistics(data, "T")                                # the points, each once
by_volume = td.statistics(data, "T_cell", weights="volume")
print(f"mean of the samples {samples.mean:.3f} K, over the volume {by_volume.mean:.3f} K")
# mean of the samples 1.393 K, over the volume 4.000 K
totals = td.integrate(data, ["T"])
assert np.isclose(by_volume.mean, totals.values["T"] / totals.measure)
assert np.isclose(by_volume.weight, 8.0) and by_volume.weight_unit == "m**3"

    The volume-weighted mean equals the integral over the measure, which integrate gives directly. With weights the population variance is m2 / W, and the sample variance treats the weights as reliability weights, as NumPy's aweights does.

    Measure how a field's values spread¶

    entropy(data, field, bins=10) is the Shannon entropy, in bits, of the field's histogram: -sum(p log2 p) over the shares of the values in bins equal bins over their range, as Viskores' Entropy filter computes it. A constant field has entropy 0, and values spread evenly over 2^k bins have entropy k.

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

values = np.random.default_rng(0).uniform(size=100_000).astype(np.float32)
cloud = td.point_cloud(np.c_[values, values, values])
cloud.fields["u"] = td.Field(td.H1(cloud), tack.array(values))

bits = td.entropy(cloud, "u", bins=8)
print(f"{bits:.3f} bits")                      # 3.000 bits: 8 equally full bins
assert abs(bits - 3.0) < 0.01

    entropy leaves out ghosts, combines pieces and takes AMR datasets and octrees as statistics does. Over pieces, the bins span the whole dataset's range. weights works as for statistics: each bin's share is the sum of its weights over the sum of all of them. See the reference.

    Label connected regions¶

    connected_components(data) finds which cells are connected through shared points, as VTK's vtkConnectivityFilter does with all regions extracted. It returns the dataset with two new fields: region on the cells and point_region on the points. Regions are numbered from 0 in the order of their lowest cell id. A point no cell uses, and a hidden cell, get -1.

    To pick one region, threshold region:

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

data = td.uniform_grid((21, 21, 21), origin=(-1, -1, -1), spacing=(0.1, 0.1, 0.1))
x = data.positions()
to_nearer_centre = np.minimum(np.linalg.norm(x - [0.5, 0, 0], axis=1),
                              np.linalg.norm(x + [0.5, 0, 0], axis=1))
data.fields["d"] = td.Field(td.H1(data), tack.array(to_nearer_centre))

blobs = td.threshold(data, "d", 0.0, 0.3)               # two separate balls of cells
labelled = td.connected_components(blobs)
regions, sizes = np.unique(labelled.fields["region"].values.numpy(), return_counts=True)
print(dict(zip(regions.tolist(), sizes.tolist())))      # {0: 48, 1: 48}

largest = regions[np.argmax(sizes)]
one_ball = td.threshold(labelled, "region", largest, largest)
assert len(regions) == 2 and one_ball.num_cells == sizes.max()

    It runs on the device, by union-find over the cells and their points. The regions are the same on every device and every run, since each is numbered by its lowest cell.

    Cluster points by distance¶

    With radius, points within that distance of each other are connected too. On a point cloud, whose cells are its points, that is Euclidean clustering, as VTK's vtkEuclideanClusterExtraction does it. With a linking length it is friends-of-friends halo finding. min_size gives the regions of fewer points the label -1, and numbers the rest again in the same order.

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
points = np.concatenate([rng.normal(0.0, 0.05, (200, 3)),       # a cluster of 200
                         rng.normal(1.0, 0.05, (300, 3)),       # a cluster of 300
                         [[5.0, 5.0, 5.0]]]).astype(np.float32)  # a stray point
cloud = td.point_cloud(points)

clusters = td.connected_components(cloud, radius=0.1, min_size=10)
labels, sizes = np.unique(clusters.fields["region"].values.numpy(), return_counts=True)
print(dict(zip(labels.tolist(), sizes.tolist())))      # {-1: 1, 0: 200, 1: 300}
assert dict(zip(labels.tolist(), sizes.tolist())) == {-1: 1, 0: 200, 1: 300}

    periodic= joins points across the faces of a periodic box, as a cosmology box needs. The points and particles page covers clustering and halo catalogues in full.

    Regions over pieces¶

    Over a PartitionedDataSet, regions are numbered across every piece of every MPI rank. Each piece labels its own cells, and pieces join the regions that meet at the points they share. With global ids on every piece, regions are numbered by their lowest global cell id, which is the whole dataset's numbering. Without them, they are numbered by their lowest (piece, cell). Filters over pieces explains pieces, ghost cells and global ids. See the reference.

    Details¶

    Pieces give the whole's answer. Where every shared cell and point has one owner, as the ghost marks arrange, statistics over pieces equal the whole dataset's up to rounding. The order of combination is fixed by the pieces' order, not by which rank finished first.

    Stored values, not functions. statistics and entropy count the values a field stores. For an H1 field of order 1 (point data), those are the point values. For a field of higher order, they are its node values, or a Bernstein basis's coefficients, and neither is a set of samples spread evenly over the cells. For integrals and true averages of any order, use integrate or project into cell data.
