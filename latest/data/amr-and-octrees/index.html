    AMR hierarchies and octrees¶

    Adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) puts fine cells where a simulation needs them and coarse cells elsewhere. Codes do it in two ways. Block-structured codes such as AMReX, Chombo and Enzo cover regions with finer boxes, level after level. Tree-based codes such as RAMSES, and the CEA codes ParaView reads as HyperTreeGrids, split single cells into children wherever more resolution is needed. Tack holds the first as an AMRDataSet and the second as an OctreeDataSet. This page explains both: how each is described, what Tack checks when you make one, and how ghost marks make every region count once. After reading it you can build either from a code's output and know which of its values a reduction counts. It assumes Data in pieces, whose ghost marks both use.

    Two ways to refine¶

    AMRDataSet (block-structured) OctreeDataSet (tree-based)
    refines boxes of cells, by a ratio per level single cells, into 2 along each axis
    holds levels, each a PartitionedDataSet of structured boxes a grid of root cells and every node of its trees
    a fine region a finer box over coarse cells a node's children
    the covered coarse data marked REFINEDCELL and REFINEDPOINT the node marked REFINEDCELL
    fields any, on each box cell data, one value per node
    VTK vtkOverlappingAMR vtkHyperTreeGrid 
    flowchart TD
    A[AMRDataSet] --> L0[Level 0: PartitionedDataSet of boxes]
    A --> L1[Level 1: finer boxes]
    L0 --> C[Coarse cells under fine boxes: REFINEDCELL]
    O[OctreeDataSet] --> R[Root grid: one tree per cell]
    R --> N[Nodes: tree, level, Morton key]
    N --> P[Refined nodes: REFINEDCELL]
    N --> F[Leaves: the finest regions]
    F -. leaves .-> D[DataSet of voxels]

    In both, a coarse value that a finer one covers stays stored and valid, but is superseded: a reduction over the whole leaves it out. That is the one rule to remember, and the ghost marks carry it.

    Block-structured AMR¶

    An AMRDataSet is a list of levels. Each level is a PartitionedDataSet of boxes, or a plain list of datasets, and each box is a dataset with a StructuredTopology. ratios[l] is how much finer level l + 1 is than level l: one whole number, or one per axis.

    import tack
import tack.dataset as td
from tack.dataset import Ghosts

tack.set_default_device("cpu")


def box(lo, cells, spacing):
    """An image box of ``cells`` cells whose first cell is at index ``lo`` of its level."""
    corner = tuple(i * spacing for i in lo)
    image = td.uniform_grid(tuple(n + 1 for n in cells), origin=corner, spacing=(spacing,) * 2)
    return td.DataSet(td.StructuredTopology(tuple(n + 1 for n in cells), offset=lo),
                      image.geometry.values)


# Two 4 x 8 boxes at level 0, and one box at level 1 across their seam, twice as fine.
amr = td.AMRDataSet([[box((0, 0), (4, 8), 1.0), box((4, 0), (4, 8), 1.0)],
                     [box((4, 4), (8, 8), 0.5)]], ratios=[2])
print(amr)

found = []
for level, piece in amr:                  # this rank's boxes, coarsest level first
    cells, points = piece.ghosts.cells.numpy(), piece.ghosts.points.numpy()
    found.append((level, int((cells & Ghosts.REFINEDCELL != 0).sum()),
                  int((points & Ghosts.REFINEDPOINT != 0).sum()),
                  int((points & Ghosts.DUPLICATEPOINT != 0).sum())))
    print(f"level {found[-1][0]}: {found[-1][1]} refined cells, {found[-1][2]} refined "
          f"points, {found[-1][3]} points a sibling owns")
assert found == [(0, 8, 15, 0), (0, 8, 15, 9), (1, 0, 0, 0)]

    The fine box covers four by four coarse cells, half in each coarse box, so each coarse box has 8 refined cells. The 15 refined points in each are the points of those cells, the edge of the refined region included. The second coarse box shares its left column of 9 points with the first, which owns them.

    The hierarchy lives in index space¶

    A box is placed in its level by cell indices alone. Its topology's offset is the index of its first cell in the level, and its point dimensions give its extent. Level 1's box above starts at cell (4, 4) of level 1, which is cell (2, 2) of level 0. Because the hierarchy needs nothing but indices, a box's geometry can be an image, a rectilinear grid or a curvilinear grid. domain is level 0's index box, (lo, hi) with hi exclusive; by default it is the bounding box of level 0's boxes.

    Tack checks the hierarchy when it is made, and refuses:

    • boxes of one level that overlap;
    • a fine box that, coarsened by the ratio, is not inside its coarser level's boxes (proper nesting);
    • image boxes of one level that do not share one spacing, one origin and one orientation, which is VTK's model (rectilinear and curvilinear boxes have no such constraint);
    • boxes that already have ghost cells.

    Each region once¶

    Making the hierarchy computes each box's ghost marks:

    • REFINEDCELL on each cell a finer box covers.
    • REFINEDPOINT on each point at whose location a finer level has a point, the edge of the refined region included. VTK has no such point bit; Tack adds it.
    • DUPLICATEPOINT on each point two sibling boxes share, owned by the lower-numbered box.

    So every region and every location belongs to exactly one box, at the finest level that holds it. Reductions over the hierarchy count each once. Filters run per box and leave refined cells out of what they make. The boxes the hierarchy holds are copies of those you gave, with these marks, sharing their topology, geometry and fields.

    Over MPI ranks, each rank passes its own boxes of each level and a world (see Blocks and worlds). The hierarchy gathers every rank's box extents when it is made, so the marks are right across ranks. amr.boxes(level) gives the index boxes of a level on every rank, each as its (lo, hi) cell indices.

    vtk_to_dataset reads a vtkOverlappingAMR, and dataset_to_vtk writes one back for images and rectilinear boxes. A hierarchy with curvilinear boxes goes to VTK as a vtkPartitionedDataSetCollection with one partitioned dataset per level. td.amr_gaussian_pulse makes VTK's Gaussian-pulse test hierarchy. In a VTK pipeline, Tack's AMR filters take a vtkOverlappingAMR whole (see VTK).

    Tree-based AMR¶

    An OctreeDataSet holds a grid of trees. The roots are an image or a rectilinear grid, 2D or 3D, and each of its cells is the root of one tree. A node splits into 2 along each axis: 8 children in 3D, 4 in 2D.

    Tack stores the trees as a linear octree, a form suited to GPUs. Every node is three numbers: its tree, its level (0 at the root), and its Morton key, its integer coordinates within the tree at its level interleaved bit by bit, x in the lowest bit. A child's key is its parent's key shifted left by the dimension, plus the child's index, x fastest. Tack sorts the nodes so that each subtree is one contiguous run, parents before their children. A node is a leaf when the next node is not its child. Positions and sizes are computed from the keys and the roots; nothing about position is stored.

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

# Two root cells side by side. The first is refined once, and its last child
# (child 7, at the far corner) is refined again.
roots = td.uniform_grid((3, 2, 2))
tree = [0] + [0] * 8 + [0] * 8 + [1]
level = [0] + [1] * 8 + [2] * 8 + [0]
key = [0] + list(range(8)) + [(7 << 3) | child for child in range(8)] + [0]
octree = td.OctreeDataSet(roots, tree, level, key,
                          fields={"level": np.array(level, np.float32)})
print(octree)                             # 2 trees, 18 nodes, 16 leaves

leaf = octree.is_leaf
assert leaf.sum() == 16 and octree.finest == 2
assert np.isclose(octree.measures()[leaf].sum(), 2.0)    # the leaves fill both roots

leaves = octree.leaves()                  # the leaves as a mesh of voxels
print(leaves.num_cells, "cells,", leaves.num_points, "points")
assert leaves.num_cells == 16 and leaves.num_points == 27 + 19 + 4

    The leaves tile the two unit cubes, so their volumes sum to 2. The nodes that are not leaves, the first root and its seventh child, are marked REFINEDCELL. So reductions count each region once, over the leaves.

    Values on every node¶

    An octree holds cell data, one value per node, refined nodes included, as the codes store them. fields maps names to one value per node in the order you give the nodes; Tack sorts them with the nodes. mask=, one value per node, hides the nodes where it is nonzero (HIDDENCELL). units= gives the fields' units, and the spatial unit is the roots'.

    Codes often write only cells, not the tree. OctreeDataSet.from_cells(roots, level, position, fields) takes each cell's level and a position inside it, as a RAMSES reader or yt gives them, over a grid of root cells. It adds the ancestors no cell gives, with NaN values.

    Leaves as a mesh¶

    octree.leaves() is the leaves as an unstructured DataSet of voxels, or pixels in 2D, with every field as cell data. Cell c is the c-th leaf in the nodes' order, and hidden leaves are marked HIDDENCELL. Corners that coincide are one point, so the cells are connected. A corner that lies on a finer neighbour's face stays where it is, a hanging point, as the tree has it. Every filter that reads cell data works on the leaves.

    Over MPI ranks, each rank holds whole trees, and world= says where the others are. vtk_to_dataset reads a vtkHyperTreeGrid with branching factor 2, and dataset_to_vtk writes one back, a mask as hidden cells.

    Counting and weighting¶

    Both kinds of data count a region once, but their cells differ in size. An octree's leaves differ in volume by a factor of 8 a level, and so do an AMR hierarchy's levels by the cube of the ratio. A plain mean over the cells weighs a tiny cell as much as a huge one. A volume-weighted mean answers a different question, and a density's volume-weighted sum is a mass. The reductions take weights= for this; Reduce shows them.

    Details¶

    What the marks leave out¶

    Mark Set on Kept? Counted by reductions?
    REFINEDCELL coarse AMR cells under a finer box; octree nodes with children yes, valid data no
    REFINEDPOINT coarse AMR points where a finer level has a point yes, valid data no
    DUPLICATEPOINT AMR points a lower-numbered sibling also holds yes no
    HIDDENCELL masked octree nodes; cells whose data is not valid yes no, and filters skip them

    Ghosts.MASKED_CELLS (hidden and refined) is what filters that make cells leave out. Ghosts.SKIPPED_CELLS and Ghosts.SKIPPED_POINTS are what reductions leave out.

    Limits¶

    AMR: contours across levels are not joined crack-free, and periodic domains, tagging, regridding and conservative interpolation are not part of Tack. Octrees: point data, coarse values computed from the leaves (what HyperTreeGrid calls EvaluateCoarse) and contours on the dual grid are not there yet.
