# tack-vis

Notes for `tack.dataset` and `tack.interop`, beside the root `CLAUDE.md`
(the kernel language, backends, `tack.arrays`, `tack.communication`). Paths
are under `src/tack/dataset/` (algorithms in `algorithms/`, the interop in
`src/tack/interop/`), tests under `tests/`.

## Devices

`tack.dataset` follows the same model: a topology, field, set or dataset has `_device` (what `same_device`/`on_inputs_device` read; `same_device` skips classes, a classmethod's first argument) and `.device`. Topologies find it from their stored arrays (`_STORED`, cached once found); `StructuredTopology` records the device it was made for (`device=`); derived `Faces`/`Edges`/`PolygonFaces`/`SizeBuckets` take theirs from what they hold (`_OnItsArrays`); spaces from their topology. Every public algorithm, topology derivation (`groups`, `faces`, `edges`, `point_links`, `cell_points`), space layout, `Set` builder, `CellLocator`, bucket helper and `for_each`/`launch_groups`/`domain_view` is wrapped in `on_inputs_device`, so it runs and allocates on its dataset's device; a new entry point that allocates needs the decorator too. `Field` and `DataSet` refuse parts on two devices; `DataSet.to_device` rebuilds the topology (`_moved`), spaces (`Space._rebuilt_on`), fields and sets there. Implicit arrays live where the arrays they hold are: `ConstantArray`, `UniformCoordinates`, `CartesianProduct` take `device=`, `_Implicit.to_device` moves one, `_operands` stores host operands beside device ones, and `_metadata` refuses two devices. See `packages/tack-vis/tests/test_dataset_devices.py` (every algorithm on a simulated second device, and on a CPU backend without f64, as Metal is: an algorithm must not hand its kernels an f64 array there; data that crosses devices as f64 goes as bits in u64).

## Positions and origins

A cell's Jacobian (`_Geometry.geometry_jacobian`, `Shape.jacobian`, `_newton`) and a field's parametric derivative (`parametric_gradient`) are formed from corners' and values' differences from the first, never sums of absolute positions or values: in f32 those lost thin cells' short edges and large values' small differences (the M6's wedge gradients up to 93x off; `test_thin_cells_keep_their_gradients_in_f32`). A dataset's positions are `origin + value` (`DataSet.origin`, three doubles on the host, `as_origin`; the values in the geometry's type, f32 or f64 where the device has it), so far coordinates keep their precision in f32. Kernels see only the values. Filters keep the origin through `carry`, `keep_marks` and `drop_marks`, so a filter that builds a `DataSet` some other way passes `origin=`. Absolute positions convert on the host in f64: implicit functions (`implicit.relative_to`: the built-ins' `_relative_to` moves their parameters, anything else is wrapped in `_Shifted`), host probe points and seeds (`relative_positions`; Tack arrays and datasets are relative to their origin, and a dataset on another one is shifted by `_moving._shifted_values`), `point_elevation`, `interpolate`'s function (`_Of.at`, compiled out at zero), `transform` (moves the origin, the linear part on the values; an image's map folded into its origin and direction, so it stays an image), `cylindrical`/`spherical` (world positions, origin zero), the VTK interop (`vtk_to_dataset(origin=)`, world points out; without one, converting to f32 chooses one where the data are far from zero for their width, `dataset.automatic_origin`, the same for a composite's pieces). Pieces whose positions meet share one origin (`partitioned.common_origin`, gathered over the world).

## Fields by name and space

A dataset's fields are a `Fields` mapping (`dataset.py`), by name and space: two fields may share a name in different spaces (VTK's point and cell arrays; the interop maps both ways without renaming). `fields[name]` is the one of that name, refused (`KeyError`, `get` and `pop` too) where several share it; `fields[name, kind_or_space]` picks by `isinstance` or by space (interned per topology, so `is`; across topologies, kind and parameters, `_same_space`). Keys are names, or `(name, space)` for a shared name, so every key is a lookup and `dict(data.fields)` round-trips through `DataSet(fields=)`, which keeps each entry under its field's own space and refuses two of a name in one. `fields[name] = f` replaces every field of the name, `fields[name, space] = f` goes beside them. A field argument is a `Field`, a name or `(name, space)` (`is_field_key`, never `isinstance(x, str)`); `selected` gives `fields=`'s keys, a name selecting all of it; message and plan names between pieces are `field_label(key)` (a string, alike on every rank: tuple keys neither sort nor pickle); output names built from a field's go through `field_name(key)`; a copy of the mapping takes a new value under `fields.key_of(key)`; writers that name arrays by kind iterate `fields.entries()`. `test_fields_by_space.py`.

## Time and temporal filters

Time is a dataset's step, not a series (the vault's temporal-filters proposal, T0): `DataSet(..., time=, time_unit=)` (`time` a host float or `None`, `time_unit` a unit of time, `units.time_unit`), and `DataSet._frame()` gives origin, spatial unit, time and time unit together, which every filter that builds a dataset passes (`DataSet(..., **data._frame())`; `keep_marks`/`drop_marks` keep them through `_keep_frame`). Pieces and AMR boxes at two times are refused (`partitioned._one_time`, compared in one unit); an octree's time is its roots'. The VTK interop maps `DATA_TIME_STEP` (the public converters wrap `_vtk_to_dataset`/`_dataset_to_vtk` and stamp the time, `_stamp`, `_time_of`). `dataset/temporal.py` holds what temporal filters (objects fed `update(data)`) share: `_Step` keeps copies of a step's fields and positions (implicit arrays as they are; `copy=False` keeps references) and its topology by reference, `_elapsed` the time between steps in a unit. Temporal filters derive `_Streamed` (`update(data)`, state per piece of a `PartitionedDataSet`, run on the step's device): `RunningStatistics` (Welford sums `_start`/`_welford`, outputs copied each step) and `TimeDerivative` (a `_Step` of the previous step, `_rate`); both refuse another mesh (`_same_mesh`: the same object, or the same kind, sizes and arrays compared on the device by `_equal`). `Advection` (T2) keeps particle state on the host in f64 (`x`, `time`, `release`, `seed` (rank << 40 for pieces), `source`, `status` in `ParticleStatus` bits), the previous step as `_kept_dataset` (a `_Step`'s copies on the step's topology), and integrates host-side RK4/Euler substeps whose velocity is `probe` of both steps blended linearly in time (`_velocities`; any meshes, routed over ranks for pieces, so particles never migrate); `inject` queues releases (`_pending`), placed at update (`_release`); `extend_paths` merges by `seed` into polylines (`_lines`; a one-point group keeps its point in no line), `Pathlines` and `Streaklines` build on them. `test_temporal.py` runs every algorithm of `test_dataset_devices.CALLS` at a time and holds the filters to NumPy.

## Units

Units are meaning, not storage (`dataset/units.py`; the vault's units proposal, phase U0): `Field(space, values, unit=None)` keeps a string in unyt's canonical spelling (`units.canonical`, cached; `^` read as `**`; `None` unknown, `"1"` is `"dimensionless"`), and the spatial unit is the geometry field's (`DataSet.unit`, `DataSet(..., unit=)`, the grid and cloud constructors' `unit=`; the origin is in it). unyt (a tack-vis dependency, pure Python) is imported on first use and its objects never leave `units.py`; the registry adds `ftUS`. Kept by `carry` (every carried field, and the output's geometry in the input's unit), `keep_marks`/`drop_marks` (`_keep_unit`, beside the origin), `to_device`, every `DataSet(..., origin=X.origin, unit=X.unit)`, and the same-quantity filters (`project`, `values_at_centers`, traces, `magnitude`, voxel means, SPH interpolation, `cell_centers`/`face_centers` in the spatial unit, `contour_value`); cylindrical/spherical an unknown spatial unit. Filters that compute a new quantity derive its unit (phase U1) with `units.derived((unit, power), ...)` (`None` in, `None` out; offset units refused), `units.delta` (a difference of °C is in K: gradients, jumps, variances), `units.named` (fold to an SI name, only where a filter names its result: `dot`, `cross`), and `_common._measure_unit`/`_time_unit`; `Statistics` carries `unit`, `weight_unit` and `unit_of(name)`, pieces' units gathered over the world. A new filter that makes a `Field` passes its unit; forgetting gives `None`, never a wrong unit. Two known spatial units are refused where positions meet (`relative_positions(where, origin, unit)`, `common_origin`, the AMR constructor; `units.check`, `units.common`; unknown passes). `convert(data, unit, field=None)` scales on the device (`_scaled`), the origin on the host; images and rectilinear grids stay implicit. Parameters take quantities (phase U2: `units.quantity` parses `"10 cm"`, `(value, unit)`, unyt/pint objects; `units.magnitude` converts, offsets included; `units.number_and_unit` for a mass or velocity that brings its unit): `_common._length` (to the spatial unit; `_spatial_unit` gathers a partitioned dataset's over the world), `_in_unit_of` (to a field's), step sizes to `_time_unit`; a plain number is never touched, and a string that names a field stays a field. `OctreeDataSet(units=)` holds its fields' units. The VTK interop maps `UNITS_LABEL` (`_units_label`, `_label`; an image has nowhere to keep one). `test_units.py` runs every algorithm of `test_dataset_devices.CALLS` with units.

## AMR and octrees

`AMRDataSet` (`dataset/amr.py`) is block-structured AMR: levels of `PartitionedDataSet`s of `StructuredTopology` boxes, each placed in its level by `offset` (cell-index box `[offset, offset + cells)`), ratios per axis between levels; the hierarchy is in index space only, so a box's geometry may be an image, a rectilinear or a curvilinear grid. The constructor gathers every rank's boxes (`boxes(level)`, `_first`), refuses overlaps, improper nesting, images off their level's lattice (VTK's model) and ghost cells, and returns copies with marks (`_marked`, a level at a time: `_refined_boxes`, and every test of box against box (siblings, overlaps, nesting) through `_meeting`, a sort and sweep on the first axis, never every pair): `REFINEDCELL` (cells under a coarsened finer box), `REFINEDPOINT` (Tack's bit 8: point `p` with `p * ratio` in a finer box's point extent) and `DUPLICATEPOINT` for points a lower-numbered sibling holds, so each region and location is owned once. `Ghosts.MASKED_CELLS` (hidden | refined) is what `visible`/`without_hidden`/`refuse_hidden` drop; `SKIPPED_CELLS`/`SKIPPED_POINTS` include the refined bits. `statistics`/`entropy` reduce `_as_pieces()`. The VTK interop maps `vtkOverlappingAMR` both ways (`_amr_to_dataset`, each box placed as it is converted, `_vtk_to_dataset(placement=)`, on the hierarchy's origin; `_amr_to_vtk`; a 2D box's missing axis is hi -1 in a `vtkAMRBox`); `amr_gaussian_pulse` is VTK's source. Ghost cells and averages (`algorithms/amr_ghosts.py`): every value a box holds but does not own is a weighted sum of values its owners hold, a `_Fill` over groups (`_Groups`: a rank's boxes on one device, their arrays end to end; a `Plan` between groups stages the sources other groups hold after the group's buffer, then one `_apply` sums each target's sources by weight, `_pick` for integers), never a block or a plan pair per box (AMR-Wind's 6,436 boxes made 326,480 launches a fill). The sources are found by kernels over a level's boxes at once (`_ghost_cells_of`, `_ghost_points_of`, `_down_cells_of`, `_down_points_of`: counts, then CSR rows; weights as integer numerators, so no device needs f64), through `_LevelBoxes`, a device class of a level's boxes binned on a uniform grid (`find`: the lowest box id holding an index); `ghost_cells(amr, n)` grows boxes within `level_domain`, rows from the first (owning) sibling, else the coarse cell (injection) or the coarse corners' owners (linear weights), else hidden; positions go through the point fill (images keep their lattice; rectilinear axes are read back, hidden points extrapolated); the result is `AMRDataSet._grown` with `_extents`, `layers` and the fills `update_ghosts` reruns (shared points of lower siblings included). `average_down` builds a fill per level, finest first: cells as sums of measure times value over sums of measure. `probe(amr, where)` is the pieces' probe over `_as_pieces()` with `levels=` (a holding rank scaled so the finer level wins ties; refined cells are dropped by `visible`), and resampling is probe onto an image (`test_amr_probe.py` against `vtkAMRResampleFilter`). `OctreeDataSet` (`dataset/octree.py`) is tree-based AMR as a linear octree: roots (an image or rectilinear grid's cells, x fastest) and every node as `tree`, `level`, `key` (Morton, x lowest; a child is its parent's key `<< d | child`, HyperTreeGrid's x-fastest child order), sorted in pre-order (`_preorder`: two stable sorts), leaves where the next node is not a child (`_leaves`), parents and whole octs checked (`_check_ancestors`), geometry by `_node_geometry` from per-tree root cells; non-leaves `REFINEDCELL`, a mask `HIDDENCELL`. `from_cells` adds ancestors (NaN values). `leaves()` is the leaves as an `UnstructuredTopology` of voxels or pixels (cell `c` the `c`-th leaf, `is_leaf` the boolean): each leaf's corners on the finest level's lattice over every root (`_leaf_corners`), stable `argsort`s by x, then y, then z, runs of equal corners numbered by a scan (`_run_starts`, `_numbered`), positions from the lattice index alone (`_lattice_points`), so shared corners have equal bits; hanging corners stay. `probe(octree, where)` dispatches to `_probe`: per point, root cell by binary searches of the axes, its code on the finest level, then the last node whose (tree, key carried to the finest level, `_carried`) is at most it, which in pre-order is the leaf holding it (`_find_leaves`), held outside the roots or off a 2D plane within the locator's `_TOLERANCE` of the leaf's diagonal; over ranks a `Routing` with one block per rank (`_probed_over_ranks`, boxes of the held trees, `_box`). `statistics`/`entropy` dispatch to `_statistics`/`_entropy` (leaves or `level=`, every rank's via `world._allgather`). Weighted statistics (`weights="volume"` or a field) live in `statistics.py` (`_weights`, `_weighted_moments`, `_merged` with W in place of n, `Statistics.weight`); entropy's weighted counts are host `_weighted_counts`. The VTK interop reads and writes `vtkHyperTreeGrid` through breadth-first descriptors (`_htg_to_octree`, `_octree_to_htg`; bit arrays unpacked by `_bits`). `test_octrees.py` holds geometry to `vtkHyperTreeGridGenerateFields`, leaves to `vtkHyperTreeGridToUnstructuredGrid`, probes to `vtkHyperTreeGridProbeFilter` and to the probe of the leaves, round trips, from_cells, statistics to NumPy, and 2 and 3 MPI ranks (`_octree_mpi.py`, statistics and probes). `test_amr.py` holds the marks to VTK's `BlankCells`, the same hierarchy as images, rectilinear and curvilinear grids, each location and region owned once, and 2 and 3 MPI ranks (`_amr_mpi.py`). `test_origin.py` runs every algorithm of `test_dataset_devices.CALLS` at zero and at a far origin, bit for bit.

## Higher orders

Higher orders (the vault's higher-order proposal, round 1): `tack.dataset.bases` holds 1D node families on [0, 1] (`gauss_lobatto`, `equispaced`, and for `L2` `gauss_legendre`; symmetric to the bit), barycentric `weights`, the Lagrange-to-Bernstein matrix (`bernstein`), and per (tensor shape, order) where each lexicographic node lives (`dof_tables`: corner, edge, face, interior, and its place); `view_order` maps a lexicographic node to an `H1` view's cell order (the quadratic shapes' at order 2). `H1(order=p, basis=)` from 3 and `L2(order=p, basis=)` from 2 (or `basis="gauss_legendre"`) are on segments, quads, pixels, hexahedra and voxels; `basis` names the family (Lagrange on `gauss_lobatto`, `equispaced` or `gauss_legendre` nodes, or `bernstein`; MFEM's BasisType, round 2 renamed it from `nodes`) and is a parameter only where it changes the basis. The views (`views._TensorBasis`, `_TensorDofs`, `_H1TensorField`, `_L2TensorField`, `_H1TensorGeometry`; one class per order, `views.of_order`) read node tables as small arrays in the field's float type (`Space.attributes(group, dtype)`), since a device class cannot index a table constant at a runtime index; a cell reads a shared edge from its lower point id (`edge_sign`) and a face in its row's frame (`face_orientation`). Order 2 on hexahedra and quads keeps `_H1Order2Field` for fields, but a curved geometry there reads as `_H1TensorGeometry` of order 2. Every field view has `value_range(c)` and `flatness(c)` (Bernstein bounds; the interior's coefficient included at order 2), and on a shape with a reference element its own nodes, numbered as `dof(c, k)`: `NODES` (a class constant; `view_class` sets it through `_node_count` where it follows the shape: corners at order 1, `NUM_QUADRATIC` at order 2, `(ORDER + 1)^d` in a tensor basis, a serendipity field's stored corners and edges, `_SerendipityField`), `node_parametric(k)` and `dof_index(c, k)` (`test_field_view_nodes.py`; the shape's `NUM_QUADRATIC` is not a field's node count); cell views have `control_box(c)` (`_CornerBox`, or a curved geometry's control points) and `curved_to_parametric` (`_CurvedNewton`), which the cell locator uses, so curved cells are bounded and inverted as they are (a vertex has a zero Newton step for it). `algorithms/subdivide.py`: `subdivide(data, level)` and the sub-cells `contour`/`slice` cut (`subdivision=`, `tolerance=`): cells whose `value_range` straddles a value, lattice points numbered as the equispaced `H1(order=level)` space's nodes, sorted, each evaluated once: a corner from the stored corner; an edge or face point from that entity's trace (`_trace_values`, `_trace_straight`, `_trace_curved`: `bases.entity_nodes`' values in a frame the corners' positions fix by `_before`), so every piece holding it gets the same bits; the rest (interiors, `L2` fields) by the refined cell every piece chooses alike (`_evaluators`: lowest global id, else first centroid by position). With ghost marks, lattice points are marked as `carry` marks made points (`_lattice_marks`) and sub-cells take their parents' (`test_subdivided_contours_give_each_piece_its_share`). Bernstein spaces (`basis="bernstein"`, `bases.BERNSTEIN`; its "nodes" the Greville points, its "weights" the binomials, its matrix to Bernstein form the identity): views mix `_BernsteinPolynomials` (tensor), `_SimplexBernstein` or `_WedgeBernstein` in before the Lagrange ones (`of_order`/`of_simplex_order`/`of_wedge_order(..., bernstein)`), and order 2 reads as a basis of any order (`H1._any_order(shape)`, also order 2 on segments; `view_order(..., basis)` the identity). `dataset/_bernstein.py`: `interpolate`/`project` fill the equispaced companion (`lagrange_companion`, the same layout) and `from_lagrange` converts, corners copied, edges and faces in their position frames (`_h1_coefficients`, `_simplex_h1_coefficients`), insides and `L2` cells in the cell's; `to_lagrange` back, and `with_lagrange_geometry` gives `interpolate` a curved Bernstein geometry's values at nodes. Traces switch with a flag (`subdivide._axis`, `_triangle_term`; `_crossings._Nodes.bern`). VTK's Bezier cells (75-80, `_lagrange.BEZIER`) map as their Lagrange twins, into Bernstein spaces; `dataset_to_vtk(higher_order="bezier")`. `test_bernstein.py` holds everything to the Lagrange twin. Serendipity (`basis="serendipity"`, `bases.SERENDIPITY`, order 2): the layout keeps points and edges only (`_layout`'s face and inside counts zero), which number exactly as `H1(order=2)`'s first values; `_space_parts` gives views `_serendipity.full_values` (the stored values, then each quad face's centre summed smallest first, `_sorted_sum4`, and each inside; cached by revision), so kernels read an ordinary order-2 field and compile nothing new (computing the centres inside the views compiled 6-12x slower); writes go through the full view's `dof_index` to the stored prefix; `stored_nodes(shape)` lists a space's stored lexicographic nodes for `carry`, `project`, `_h1_indices` and the VTK quadratic cells (`_lagrange.QUADRATIC`, 22-26). `test_serendipity.py` holds it to quadratics and to VTK's quadratic cells. Element-to-DOF tables (`H1(..., dofs=table)`, a row per cell in lexicographic or lattice node order, corners at the cell's points, checked by `spaces._checked_table`): views put `views._TableDofs` (`dof_index` a lookup in `dof_table`) before the space's own (`with_table`, `H1._tabled`) and take no edge or face mixins; the space reads as any-order at 2 too; `interpolate` writes points, then each node by its first (cell, node) entry (`_into_table`); `carry` lists both spaces' nodes lexicographically (`_node_indices`' `node_of`) and outputs numbering by entity; `with_lagrange_geometry` renumbers a table geometry for node placement; `_layout()` refuses. `test_dof_table.py` holds table fields to their twins numbered by entity. Exact crossings (`algorithms/_crossings.py`, the cuts' `exact=True`): `_refined(..., cut=(name, levels))` gives the refined dataset a `Crossings` (`refined._crossings`), and `carry` turns an `Interpolate` from it into one with `exact` (`Crossings.located`, a `_Located`): each crossing's holding cell (`_holding`, the first by rank among the refined cells holding both ends, via each point's run of keys), the smallest entity holding its sub-edge (`_ends`, from `bases.entity_coordinates`), walked from the end first in the entity's frame (`_oriented`), and its root by `ROOT_STEPS` Illinois steps on the cut field less the nearest level (`_roots`), bracketed by the lattice values; `_apply` then writes each field's source there (`_field_rows` through `_Nodes.term`, the trace's terms on an edge, quad or triangle face; `_curved_rows`, `_straight_rows`), and `carry` does the same to a geometry the filter passed, recomputing clip's centroids. `test_exact_crossings.py` (vertices on the level, carried fields, pieces bit for bit). `clip` and `threshold` of the same data go through `split_by_range`: `_by_range` sorts cells by `value_range` into kept whole (`_keep_cells`, curved and high-order as they are), dropped, and cut (`_refined`, then the linear filter on the sub-cells), returning `PartitionedDataSet([whole, part])`; a cell field never cuts. The whole part owns the whole cells' corners on the seam: `_refined(..., elsewhere)` adds the point field `SEAM` (1 at lattice corners on them), carried through the cut as a field, since marks there would make `carry_marks` drop them (`_unplaceable`: copies without ghost cells), and `_seam_marked` turns it into `DUPLICATEPOINT` on the cut part (`test_clip_high_order.py`, over pieces too). `L2(layout=)` (the vault's coefficient-layout proposal): a tensor basis' values in `"blocks"` (`offsets[c] + n`) or `"cells"` (`n * num_cells + c`, `views._L2CellsField`), `"auto"` resolving from the topology's device when read (`L2.layout`; cells for order 3 and up on a GPU, `spaces._on_gpu`), `TACK_L2_LAYOUT` overriding it; every `L2` view has `dof_index`, and what indexes `L2` storage outside the views (node views, `project`, `carry._copy_laid_out`, `ghosts._keep_cells`, the MFEM interop through `space.indices()`) follows the layout; `DataSet.to_device` transposes when it changes (`_relaid`); `space.blocks(values)` reads per cell. CI's `l2-cells` job runs the dataset tests cell-fastest. See `test_l2_layout.py`. Simplices of any order (round 2, equispaced only): `bases.simplex_lattice` (`(i, j, k)/p`, `i + j + k <= p`, the first axis fastest), Silvester's basis (`simplex_basis` on the host, `views._SimplexPolynomials` on the device from per-corner factor tables, `of_simplex_order` setting `ORDER` and `NODES`), `simplex_bernstein` for `value_range`/`flatness`, `dof_tables` for simplices (a triangle face's `(a, b)` from its corner 0 toward 1 and 2, placed in the face's row by `_SimplexFaces.simplex_face_dof` through `face_position`, numbered `U` fastest); `H1`'s layout counts `(p-1)(p-2)/2` per triangle face or triangle, `(p-1)(p-2)(p-3)/6` per tetrahedron; any other node family above order 2 is refused (`_refuse_simplex_nodes`), and `L2` on simplices is always blocks. Subdivision takes all-simplex meshes (`_cell_family`): Freudenthal's sub-simplices (`bases.simplex_subdivision`, the Kuhn simplices of the ordered coordinates), lattice points from `bases.lattice_ijk`, triangle faces' points by `_trace_simplex_values`/`_trace_simplex_straight` in their corners' order by position; a curved simplex geometry (order 2's quadratic, or `views._H1SimplexGeometry` from order 3, with `_SimplexNewton` reading its nodes once and a Bernstein `control_box`) by `_trace_simplex_curved` from its own entity nodes. See `test_simplex_order.py`. Wedges of any order (equispaced): `bases.wedge_lattice` (the triangle's lattice in each of `p + 1` layers, the triangle fastest), the basis Silvester's over five barycentric coordinates (`views._WedgePolynomials`: the triangle's three, the segment's `1 - z` and `z`), `wedge_bernstein` the two factors' Kronecker product, `dof_tables` by `_classify` (by geometry: corners, edges, triangle and quad faces, interior); `_WedgeDofs` places a triangle face's values by `_SimplexFaces` and a quad face's by `_TensorFaces`; `_H1WedgeGeometry` with `_WedgeNewton`; interior nodes by `_TableInteriorNodes`; subdivision into `bases.wedge_subdivision`'s sub-wedges (Freudenthal's triangles in layers), the trace kernels branching on a face's corner count (`_face_stride` the face rows' length). The VTK Lagrange interop takes triangles and tetrahedra (VTK's recursive node order matched to the lattice by `GetParametricCoords`, `_lagrange._simplex_vtk_index`), and the MFEM interop simplices (closed-uniform values copied by reference coordinates, `_simplex_lattice_of`; any other basis evaluated at the equispaced lattice). The MFEM interop permutes values of any order on matching nodes, and MFEM's positive bases' coefficients into Bernstein spaces, or with `dofs="table"` keeps MFEM's values and numbering as an `H1(dofs=table)` space (`_table_values`) (`_nodes_of`, by collection name; `_lexicographic` matches MFEM's nodes by coordinates) and evaluates others; `mfem_grid_function` goes back. `interop/vtk/_lagrange.py` reads and writes VTK Lagrange cells (`dataset_to_vtk(..., higher_order=True)`). Reading takes every higher-order type Tack has a shape for, mixed in one grid (`to_dataset`: per (type, node count) its nodes matched to Tack's by VTK's parametric coordinates and copied, `_node_matrix`; nodes a VTK cell lacks from VTK's own `InterpolateFunctions`, exactly; `vtkCubicLine`'s coordinates are on [-1, 1]); `test_vtk_higher_order.py` holds VTK's higher-order test data to VTK, with `TACK_VTK_DATA`. See `test_high_order.py`.

A space is also a `for_each` domain: `DataSet.node_views(space)` gives entity views (a `_Points` kind for points, edges, quad faces, cells) with a node-dof mixin (`_PointDof`, `_EdgeDof`, `_FaceDof`, `_InteriorDof`, `_CornerDof`, `_OffsetDof`, `_CellDof`: `node_count(i)`, `node_dof(i, j)`) and a node-position mixin (`_MeanPosition` on a straight geometry, `_StraightNodePosition` at order 3 and up, `_GeometryAtDof` on a geometry of the space's layout, `_CurvedNodePosition` on a curved geometry of another order (its trace on each edge and quad face, copied from the cells by `_entity_traces`; a triangle face's trace in its canonical frame, its corners by position, `_copy_triangle_traces`, read by `_CurvedTriangleNodePosition` in the geometry's Lagrange (Silvester) or Bernstein basis), `_CornerPosition`, `_CenterPosition`; an edge's and a quad face's from its first point by position and in `_square_frame`, never its stored order, so pieces get the same bits; the order mixins' constants are `NODE_ORDER`/`NODE_COUNT`, `of_node_order`, since a cell node view is the geometry's view, whose `ORDER` may differ), added through `domain_view(..., extra, **attributes)`; `interpolate` is one kernel over them (`_at_space_nodes`), its first-writer path kept only for an `L2` geometry's shared nodes.

## Topologies

A `StructuredTopology` (2D or 3D) derives its faces and edges by arithmetic (`Faces._derive_structured`, `Edges._derive_structured`: one pass over the cells, each local face's or edge's id from (i, j, k) and per-shape tables, `_structured_face_tables` reading side 1's orientations once from the sorted derivation of a tiny grid), numbered exactly as the sort numbers them (`_before`); any other topology emits, sorts and matches (`_derive_sorted`). `test_structured_faces_and_edges_are_the_general_derivations` holds the two to identical arrays.

An `UnstructuredTopology`'s `types`, `offsets` and `connectivity` may be implicit arrays (`ConstantArray`, `CountingArray`), kept as given (`_ids_of`, `_as_field`); `uniform_cells` makes the types and offsets of cells all of one shape, and a topology whose types are a `ConstantArray` groups without the type histogram (`_gathered`). On the CPU a stored types array is counted by chunks into private tables and summed (`_type_counts`: `_count_types`, `_sum_type_counts`), with no atomics: one atomic counter per type put every worker on one counter when the types are equal (34 ms for 1M cells against 2.7 on one thread); GPUs keep the atomic histogram. Filters with one output shape emit them; code that reads a topology's arrays must accept an implicit array (index it in a kernel; `numpy()`, `np.asarray`, `materialize` on the host; `size_of`, never `.shape`). The VTK interop maps them to `vtkConstantArray`/`vtkAffineArray` (`_to_vtk_types`, `_to_vtk_ids`, `_from_vtk`). See `test_implicit_topology.py`.

## Point clouds

`td.point_cloud` makes implicit vertex cells, choosing an origin for f64 positions far from zero (`automatic_origin`: more than 100 times their width). `PointLocator` (`point_locator.py`) bins the points over their box and sorts them by bin with `bucket_order` (no atomics, each bin in index order, so queries are bit-reproducible); `_PointGrid` is its device class (`span`, `cell`, `bin`, `first`/`end`/`point`), `neighbors` counts then fills CSR, `nearest` searches rings of bins (`_nearest_into`) with the k best in private arrays (`_PRIVATE_K`, 32), or for a larger k in the output's rows (`_nearest_in_rows`), ties by index; every distance that decides something (membership, the k nearest, `_hook_near`, voxels' nearest point) goes through `squared_length`/`grid.distance2`, named products no GPU fuses, so those answers are the CPU's on every backend (a fused `d.dot(d)` flipped voxel ties on CUDA, HIP and Level Zero); the locator has its dataset's `_device`; the topology keeps one (`cached_point_locator`, `_point_locator`), refused when stale (`serves`). `voxel_downsample`, `outliers` (marks, not removal), `point_normals`, `connected_components(radius=)` (`_hook_near`: pairs p > q through the bins, beside the cell-point hooks), `deposit` (entries per point, argsort and runs: no atomics) and `sph_interpolate`/`sph_density` (`_Kernel`, kind and dimension compiled in; scatter form to targets, gather for density) are on it; `test_point_clouds.py` holds them to brute force and to VTK's vtkFiltersPoints. A periodic box (`periodic=`, `periodic_box`) sets `_PointGrid.PERIODIC` (compiled in): `bin` wraps, `span` is at most a box wide, `offset(p, x)` is to the nearest image, `ring_bin` takes each wrapped bin once; `serves(data, periodic)` and `cached_point_locator(data, periodic)` match the box; `check_radius` refuses half a box. Every kernel measures through `grid.offset`. `halo_catalog` (`algorithms/halo_catalog.py`) sorts particles by label, unwraps each halo about its first particle, sums every pair's potential up to `_EXACT` particles and prunes larger halos (`_pruned`: Viskores' MxN with per-particle bounds against far bins' boxes, `_bounds`); `test_halos.py` holds it to Viskores' cases and brute force. Point clouds in pieces (`algorithms/ghost_points.py`): `ghost_points(pds, radius=|k=)` appends copies (`_ghosted`, which `connected_components` and `outliers` call too, keeping each point's identity `(owner << 40) | index` and the copies' `Plan`, which `update_ghosts` runs): by radius, each block's points within the radius of every box (`_within`), by k, each point's local k nearest bounding the distance, queries to the boxes within it, the k best fetched (`_nearest`); in a periodic box the copies are images (shift codes, `_SHIFTS`), so per-piece filters run without `periodic`, and a piece may be its own neighbour (`Plan` allows a block to send to itself). `connected_components(pds, radius=)` labels the ghosted pieces and joins each copy's region to its owner's (`_components_near`); both piece paths share `_joined_regions` (only regions that cross pieces gathered, the rest numbered by `sort_by_key` over the blocks, `min_size` by owned points). `voxel_downsample`, `halo_catalog` (`_catalog_pieces`, `mbp_piece`) sort by voxel or label over the blocks and reduce in (piece, index) order; `deposit` sums the pieces' grids (`_deposit_pieces`, copies skipped). `test_point_pieces.py` holds every one to the whole, on two devices and on 2 and 3 MPI ranks (`_point_pieces_mpi.py`).

## Algorithms

`merge_points(data, tolerance=0)` (`algorithms/merge_points.py`) runs the same union-find over points alone: `_hook_near` through the point locator's bins at the tolerance (0 joins equal coordinates), so each set's root is its smallest point; roots are numbered by a scan, the connectivity renumbered, and `carry` takes points by `Take(roots)`, cells by `Same()`. A polyhedral topology's faces are renumbered and matched again by point set (`polyhedra._match_rings`, as `from_cell_faces` matches rings: `_merged_polyhedra`), so the one-sided copies of a face that pieces each stored become one face with both cells (OpenFOAM's processor boundaries), each cell's side `old ^ copy`, the first copy kept (`faces=Take`); copies wound the same way or a face with two cells on a side are refused. Polygonal and polyline topologies are rebuilt on the merged loops and lines. Meshes written without merging their points (the `eta` pieces) need it before matching across pieces. `vtk_to_dataset(grid, merge_points=)` merges before reading (`_merged_grid`, through `_merged_ids`), so the polyhedra's faces match once and `polyhedral="split"` decides owners on the merged points; merging the split pieces afterwards left points owned twice or nowhere (the AhmedML mesh). `connected_components(data)` (`algorithms/connected_components.py`) is union-find on the device over the cells and their points (`_hook`: each cell-point entry `atomic_min`s the larger root under the smaller; `_jump`: pointer jumping; until no hook changes anything), regions numbered by their lowest cell through a scan of the roots; `region` on cells, `point_region` on points, -1 for hidden cells and unused points. Over pieces each piece labels locally, shared points' copies (global ids or positions, `_matching.match`) give edges between (piece, region) nodes, gathered and joined on the host, numbered by the lowest owned cell's global id (or (piece, cell)).

`voxelize` (`algorithms/voxelize.py`): per voxel of an image (a `StructuredTopology` over `UniformCoordinates`), `samples` points a side at its sub-voxels' centres (`_samples`, through the coordinates' `get_ijk`, oriented images too, moved to the data's origin), probed in batches of `_BATCH` samples (`probe` of a vertex dataset `_vertices(points, like)`, so any data probe takes), then per voxel the share inside (`_fractions`) and each field's mean over its samples inside (`_means`, in sample order, no atomics); empty voxels `HIDDENCELL`. The exact version (cells clipped against voxels) is to come.

`cell_quality` (`algorithms/cell_quality.py`) is Verdict's measures generalized: per shape with a reference element, tables made once from its ideal cell (`_samples`: each corner's parametric point and edge directions, the ideal normalization and inverse corner matrix; a hexahedron's center too) as device arrays (`_tables`), one kernel (`_reference_quality`) taking `geometry_jacobian` at each sample, so order 2 works; pyramids by their four tetrahedra (`_pyramid_quality`), polyhedra and polygons by their fans (`_polyhedron_quality`, `_polygon_quality`), edges by table, sides or segments. `test_cell_quality.py` holds it to `vtkMeshQuality` (VTK 9.7.1's hexahedron edge ratio and wedge condition number differ and are checked by brute force instead).

`integrate` (`algorithms/integrate.py`) is Gauss quadrature in each cell's reference element through its own Jacobian (`quadrature`: tensor rules, collapsed (Duffy) onto triangles, tetrahedra and wedges; a pyramid's element is the unit cube, its map collapsing the top), points enough for the field's order and the geometry's (`_points_per_axis`), the measure element `_element` (|det J|, |J0 x J1|, |J0|); per-cell sums of owned cells (`owned_values`), pieces by `statistics._over_pieces`. `cell_geometry` of a curved geometry uses its `cell_measures`. `test_integrate.py` holds it to closed forms, tack-examples' MFEM ports to MFEM's integrals.

`feature_edges` (`algorithms/feature_edges.py`) works from each 2D cell's own loop of points (`_ROUND` corner orders, or a polygonal topology's `loops()`), not from `faces()`, which refuses edges of three or more cells: sides as (low, high) keys with their cells and Newell normals (`_sides`), sorted into runs by `bucket_order`/`run_offsets`, each run classified as VTK's `vtkFeatureEdges` does (`_classify`: a two-cell edge is a feature only when features are asked for, `<=` the angle's cosine), then compacted into line cells on the same points. `test_feature_edges.py` matches VTK edge for edge (by nearest input point, since VTK renumbers).

`clip` of a polyhedral topology (`algorithms/_clip_polyhedra.py`, not polygonal ones yet): each face is clipped once from its stored walk (`_face_sizes`/`_face_keys`: kept points as `(a, a)`, strict crossings as `(low, high)`, under three points gone), each cut cell's iso-polygons come from `contour`'s López tracing (`contour._lopez_polygons`, factored out of `_polyhedral_contour`; wound toward lower values, out of the kept part, so never flipped; `inside` clips negated values), points are one per run of names (`_point_runs`, an `Interpolate` from the end first by position, `_common._first_end`; the caps' names put back in (low, high) id order, `_cap_names`), and kept cells (a point strictly inside) take their clipped faces and caps. `test_clip_polyhedra.py` holds volumes to the shape clip's.

## Particle advection

Advection on a GPU runs in launches of a bounded number of steps (`_advection._launches`: the first `_FIRST_STEPS`, then sized to `_LAUNCH_SECONDS` at the last launch's pace; the CPU one launch, `_CHUNKED_ON_CPU` for the tests), so no launch reaches a driver's reset limit; a pause after a whole step keeps position, time, steps, status, `moved` (a step taken in this call, for hand-offs) and `hints` (the last cell found, since a GPU may round the hint and the search apart), and a step after a push-out starts afresh (`stage`, `sub`, `div`); `test_launches_of_a_few_steps_change_nothing` holds launches of 1 and 7 steps to one, bit for bit. `integrator="rk45"` (`_RK45`, Cash-Karp as `vtkRungeKutta45`) adapts each particle's step by VTK's controller (`tolerance`, `min_step`, `max_step`; `_adaptive` checks them, `_parameters` converts quantities); the kernel keeps a particle's current step and path length in `hs` and `travelled` (`_run`'s last return, `(hs, travelled)`, and `_Particles.step`/`travelled` over pieces), so launches and pieces go on with them; `max_length` ends a path at that length (the length unit's last step lands on it; a step shorter than a thousandth of the next is not taken). An RK45 push-out that moves the particle less than `min_step` (times the speed in the time unit) ends it `SPATIAL_BOUNDS`: at a wall the flow turns back from, stage points swing out at every trial size or stage directions cancel, and it crawled by ulps to the step limit (`test_rk45_ends_at_a_wall_it_cannot_get_past`; the aneurysm's 86 stalled paths of 20,000 to 2). `advect`/`streamlines` resume from `advect`'s output (`_run(..., resume=)`; the kernel skips particles outside, at zero velocity, handed on or at the step limit, and clears an earlier call's `TERMINATE`), and over a `PartitionedDataSet` run in rounds (`algorithms/_particles.py`): route each particle in flight to the lowest piece whose own cell holds it (boxes by `all_gather`, each candidate's locator, a reply, then the state), advance with `handoff` (the kernel stops a particle after a whole step that ends in a ghost cell, `HANDOFF` 128, only after a step in that run so nothing bounces), repeat until `all_reduce` says none is in flight. Routing is `tack.communication.Routing` (shared with `probe` over pieces, whose `holds` ranks 2 where the piece's cell holds the point, 1 within the locator's tolerance: `CellLocator._find_holding`, from `_MeshSearch.find_ranked` or the cell-side ranks, and boxes grown by the tolerance, `probe._held_box`). States live on the host between rounds; coordinates and times travel as f64 bits in u64 arrays. `streamlines` keeps each round's trace as a stretch (`seed`, `segment`, first point `DUPLICATEPOINT` after the first). `test_particles_across_pieces.py` checks pieces against the whole mesh, also under MPI.

## Polyhedra, polygons and cell location

Polyhedra have no reference element, so values inside them are linear on *sub-tetrahedra*: each face fanned from its points' mean, the fans from the cell's points' mean (both in value too, so linear fields are exact; `docs/vis-guide/design/polyhedra.md` section 13). Cell gradients are Green-Gauss over the fanned faces (`_polyhedral_gradients`, one walk per face); `cell_centers`/`values_at_centers` give the points' mean. The cell view locates and the field view evaluates, since a field view has no positions and a device function cannot call another view's methods: `views._PolyhedralLocation` (`locate`/`locate_from` give `(t, pc, inside)`, a sub-tetrahedron, its coordinates and whether it holds the point, which callers rank first; `cell_point`, `face_point`, `tet_jacobian`) on polyhedral domain views, and `_PolyhedralPointField`/`_PolyhedralCellField` (`tet_value`, `tet_parametric_gradient`), which `Space.mixin_for(key, shape)` picks by shape (`_H1Field.value` names shape functions a `Polyhedron` lacks, and every method of a view is resolved). A `CellLocator` records its topology and geometry and the geometry's `_revision` (`serves(data)`); `probe` and advection take one for any dataset of that topology and geometry and refuse a stale one, and without one use the locator the topology keeps (`cached_locator`, `_Topology._locator`, one at a time), so repeated probes build the bins once; a geometry of the same positions (a temporal filter's copy of a step, new arrays of unchanged values) takes it rebound (`holds_positions_of`, compared on the device; `rebound`: boxes and bins shared, the search made again from its positions). `CellLocator.find` returns `Locations(cells, pcs, tets)` on every dataset (`tets` None except on polyhedra), searching from the points where `tack.dataset.by_id` can read cells by id (`_MeshSearch.find` over `cell_bins()`, a two-level grid as Viskores' `CellLocatorTwoLevel`: coarse bins of about `_CELLS_PER_BIN` (32) cells, each a uniform grid of leaves sized by its count, about `_CELLS_PER_LEAF` (8; Viskores' 2 entered each of the aneurysm's tetrahedra 34 times) a leaf, rounded (Viskores truncates, which left a small even mesh's bins one leaf each), `leaves()` their dims and first leaf; `CellLocator(data, levels=1)` is one uniform grid of about `density` bins a cell, every bin a leaf (Viskores' `CellLocatorUniformBins`), for small even meshes, a point's leaf found from its coarse coordinate by `by_id._leaf` as the build places cells, so a point within a cell's widened box is in one of its leaves; ranked as the cell-side search ranks; `_PointValues`/`_CellValues.value(mesh, e, kind, pc, tet)` for probe and advection, which share it; a curved geometry and fields of other spaces through each shape group's own view, `by_id._ByShape` with `local` each cell's index in its group (a structured grid's index vector): `_CurvedCells.locate` (round 1's `curved_to_parametric` and `position`, `_MeshSearch.CURVED`) and `_ViewValues`, with one-shape variants `_OneCurvedShape`/`_OneShapeValues` called directly, since the eight-way dispatch took minutes to compile) and from the cells elsewhere, or for a fresh locator's first query of fewer points than a quarter of its cells and moves the cell point of a polyhedron not star-shaped from it into the cell's kernel (`_not_star_shaped`, then `_kernel_points`: Newton on the soft minimum of the fan triangles' clearances; the point as weights on the cell's points, `w_j = 1/n + d_j . G^-1 q`, so linear fields stay exact; `moved_cell_points`, read by `_cell_point`, `_PolyhedralMesh.moved` and probe's `_evaluate_in_moved_tets`), skipping cells with no kernel (tangled, inside out, folded faces: `starts` -2, `_SKIPPED`, left out of both searches, with a warning) and refusing polygons not star-shaped from it; the advection kernel's `_PolyhedralMesh` is built from the same mixins. Advection first tries the cell a particle was last found in (`_MeshSearch.find_from`, `_well_inside`): a tetrahedron, hexahedron, wedge or voxel holding the point by `shapes.WELL_INSIDE` (1e-3 parametric, a hundred times `HOLD_TOLERANCE`) is the only cell of a non-overlapping mesh holding it, so the search would return it with the same coordinates; a polyhedron is tried in its sub-tetrahedra (`_well_inside_fan`, `FANNED`: the search's own `locate`, taken where the holding sub-tetrahedron's four weights are each at least `WELL_INSIDE`); anything else (pyramids, surfaces, polygons, polylines, points near a face) searches the bins. `_MeshSearch.PREVIOUS_CELL = 0` turns it off; `test_trying_the_last_cell_first_changes_nothing` holds the traces bit for bit. It made streamlines 4-5x faster on real meshes. `neighbors=True` on `advect`/`streamlines` (and over pieces) then tries the last cell's face neighbours by the same rule (`_MeshSearch.NEIGHBORS`, compiled in; `CellLocator.cell_neighbors()`, `_MAX_FACES` ids a cell from `faces().sides`, -1 for none, kept; `None` for surfaces and polyhedra): 1.5-2x faster streamlines on the aneurysm and the M6, bit for bit (`test_walking_to_the_last_cells_neighbours_changes_nothing`). Polygons locate in the triangles fanning them from their cell point (`views._PolygonLocation`, `t = 2k`, `pc = (0, w_a, w_b)`, so `_PolyhedralPointField.tet_value` reads them unchanged) and polylines on their segments (`_PolylineLocation`, `_PolylinePointField`); `by_id` has `_PolygonMesh` and `_PolylineMesh`, and every mesh says `NO_REFERENCE`, which the search and evaluation branch on. VTK 9.7.1's `vtkPolyhedron` is no reference for these (wrong `Derivatives` and `EvaluatePosition`; see section 13). See `test_polyhedra.py`, `test_probe.py`, `test_flow.py`, and `benchmarks/polyhedra_compare.py` for the cost against hexahedra.

## Partitioned data, ghosts and global ids

A `PartitionedDataSet` (`dataset/partitioned.py`) is an ordered list of `DataSet`s, each with its own device and no `_device` of its own. Per-piece work takes a piece and the caller iterates; only algorithms whose answer needs more than one piece take the container (`extract_surface`: each piece's surface less the faces two pieces hold unmarked, matched by `generate_global_ids`' machinery, `_surface_of_pieces`; `statistics`, `entropy`, by field name, each piece reduced on its device, the parts gathered over `pds.world` and combined in global piece order, `statistics._over_pieces`; `fields_of` checks the name). `split` cuts a structured grid through `carry`. `carry` keeps an `H1` field above order 1 (and a curved geometry) at its order and nodes under a `Same`/`Take` cell map (`_carry_h1`: every cell's nodes' places by `dof_index` in both spaces, the first (cell, node) entry of each output node writing it, from the source cell's same node); under any other map it brings the corner values. Ghost marks are `DataSet.ghosts` (`dataset/ghosts.py`, `Ghosts`: u8 per cell and per point in VTK's `vtkGhostType` bits, moved by `to_device`, mapped to and from `vtkGhostType` by the VTK interop); every shared cell and point has one owner, the other copies are ghosts, and reductions count only owned values (`owned_values`). Filters carry the marks through `carry` (`ghosts.carry_marks`): an output cell takes its source cell's (`Take`/`Pieces`), a kept point its own, an `Interpolate` point its edge's smallest point's by position (`_before`, lexicographic); `contour`, `slice` and `clip` interpolate a crossing from that same end (`_common._first_end`, ids only between coincident points); contour and slice make a crossing whose end has the level, or that lies within half an ulp of an end relative to the edge's largest coordinate (`s + d == s`), that end (`_common._end_hit`; public as `td.edge_crossing`, which returns `(first, second, weight)`), so no cut has two points at one position: a symmetry plane's points at y = 1e-45 gave each edge leaving them its own point there, and `generate_global_ids` cannot match coincident points by position (clip does as VTK's `vtkTableBasedClipDataSet`: crossings named by their edges, so a clip through mesh points leaves coincident points and cells of no volume, which `merge_points` merges, as `vtkCleanGrid`; collapsing them exactly needs polyhedra beside shapes, a third of the table patterns fitting no single shape), so pieces sharing an edge compute it, and the values carried there, to the same bits and an average point its cell's (`Interpolate.average_cells`, clip's centroids), a cell that is an input face its one cell's or, with two sides, its smallest point's (`_faces_marks`), an edge or a cell with no source cell its smallest point's (`_owned_by_points`). With at least one ghost layer the piece owning a point holds every cell around it, which is why this gives the whole's output (`test_filters_give_each_piece_its_share_of_the_wholes_output`). Pieces with duplicate points and no duplicate cells (no ghost layers) cannot place what is made on edges and faces or kept for a kept cell (`_unplaceable`), so those outputs get `_ghosts_lost`, a `ghosts._Lost` with a reason per kind (`cells`, `points`) that `owned_values` names for the kind it reduces: a filter whose cells came one each from input cells (`carry_marks`' `from_cells`: not faces or edges) keeps their marks and loses only its points', so cell reductions (`integrate`, statistics of cell fields) go on and point ones refuse (`test_outputs_that_lost_only_their_points_marks_reduce_over_cells`); `extract_surface` loses both. It is truthy when either is lost, kept by `to_device`, and cleared by setting `ghosts` (to `None` too). A filter that builds a `DataSet` outside `carry` must call `keep_marks` or `drop_marks`. What a filter is written with is public, so users extend the built-ins with it: `td.carry` and its maps (`Same`, `Take`, `Pieces`, `Interpolate`), `keep_marks`/`drop_marks`, `visible`, `owned_values`, `Set.from_flags` and `td.units` (`__all__`: quantities as parameters, derived units). `test_public_filters.py` rewrites built-ins (a field, a subset, a new geometry, face and cell kernels, new cells, a reduction over pieces) with public names only, checked by its first test, and holds every part of each result to the built-in's; a built-in that comes to need something private belongs there with the export it needs (the vault's "Extensibility audit - findings"). Hidden cells are never processed: `_keep_cells` clears them (`without_hidden`), the other new-dataset filters start from `visible(data, *field_arguments)`, and chosen face or edge ids and reused locators are refused (`refuse_hidden`). `split(..., ghost_layers=n)` adds layers (`_block_ghosts`); `ghost_cells(pds)` (`algorithms/ghost_cells.py`) adds one to unstructured or polyhedral pieces (all of one kind; polyhedra sent with their outward faces, `_outward_rings`, the receiver's topology rebuilt by `_from_rings`, `_polyhedra_with`) through `tack.communication`, on the host apart from the exchanges: boundary points (`faces().boundary()`) keyed by a hash of their exact positions (`_position_keys`), `sort_by_key` over the blocks, matched there (`_pairs`), each point's identity `(owner << 40) | index` with the lowest piece as owner (`_owners`; VTK's rule too, `test_ghost_cells_are_vtks`), cells around shared points sent to each neighbour (`_ghost_parts`) and appended, points deduplicated by identity (`_appended`); it keeps two `Plan`s (`_ghost_plans`, points from their owners, cells from their senders) that `update_ghosts(pds, *fields)` runs. `layers=n` first works out each piece's cells in n rounds of identities only (`_needed`: frontier identities requested, cells within n steps of each piece's boundary offered under their points' identities, `_offers`, matched by `_matching.match`), then sends them in one exchange. Structured pieces take `_structured_ghosts.py`: pieces tiling a box of cells (`topology.offset`) grow to their blocks plus n cells, cell owners by block, point owners the lowest block, values placed and filled through two `Plan`s built by arithmetic (the `_ghost_plans` too), uniform coordinates moved (`split._block_geometry`), rectilinear axes gathered, explicit positions a point field. `data.global_ids` (`dataset/global_ids.py`, `GlobalIds`: i64 per point and per cell) is carried like the marks (`carry_ids` in `carry` for `Same`/`Take` maps, not `Pieces` or faces/edges as cells; `keep_marks` keeps them) and mapped to VTK's `GlobalIds` attributes; `generate_global_ids(pds)` numbers owned entities by `exclusive_scan` and brings copies' ids from owners by a `Plan` (ghost_cells' kept plans, or matching: points by position, all points when any piece has duplicates, boundary points otherwise; cells by their points' ids), equal to `vtkGenerateGlobalIds` on pieces without ghost cells, keeping the marks' owners on ghosted ones. On outputs that lost their marks (`_ghosts_lost`; `carry_marks` keeps cells' marks from source cells there) it matches every point and every unmarked cell (`_matched_cells`, by points' ids, lowest piece), marks them and clears the record: the remedy `owned_values`' refusal names, exact where ghost-cell-free pieces' filters cannot be (OpenFOAM's one-cell refinement layers' bridge edges, threshold's kept shared points). Cells a slice made from a ghost cell are triangulated in each piece's own numbering and do not match their owner's, so it raises there, as on marked pieces. The matching (`dataset/_matching.py`: `match`, `owners`, `plan`, `from_owners`, identities `(owner << 40) | index`; `match_positions` with a `tolerance` bins points into cells a little over two tolerances wide, each point at home in its own and visiting the others its box reaches (`_match_near`, `_near_pairs`: a pair is found in each one's home), and `settled` swaps identities between partners until chains of near copies agree; `generate_global_ids`, `ghost_cells` and `merge_pieces` take `tolerance=`) is shared with `ghost_cells`, which carries global ids and matches by them when every piece has them. `merge_pieces(pds, root=0)` (`algorithms/merge_pieces.py`) gathers the pieces onto the root rank's first block: structured pieces whose owned blocks (from the marks) tile a box give the box's `StructuredTopology`, every value placed by its global index; anything else goes through `generate_global_ids` (structured pieces converted with their marks set aside, so hidden cells survive) and gives an `UnstructuredTopology` ordered by cell id. `StructuredTopology(..., offset=)` is the grid index of its first point in a larger grid (VTK's extent's start; `split` and `vtk_to_dataset` set it), and `tetrahedralize` alternates by the whole grid's parity with it and ranks other cells' corners by global id when there are global ids, so pieces split shared faces alike (`test_conforming_pieces.py`). `PartitionedDataSet(partitions, world=None)` holds this rank's pieces and its `World`; `remove_ghosts` drops duplicate and hidden cells but keeps owned points (`keep_points=` of `_keep_cells`), structured blocks staying structured. No mapping helpers or parallel field types: Tack is a functionality layer, not a convenience one. See `test_partitioned.py` and the vault's partitioned-data proposal for the later phases (global ids, ghost generation, MPI).

## VTK cell grids

VTK cell grids (`interop/vtk/_cellgrid.py`): `vtk_to_dataset(vtkCellGrid)` reads each cell type's cells on the shape attribute's corners (VTK's DG corner orders are its classic ones), copies `C1` attributes (shared to `H1`, per cell to `L2`) and resamples every other `HGRAD`/Bezier basis through VTK's own evaluator (`evaluate`: `vtkCellGridEvaluator.InterpolateCellParameters` at Tack's equispaced nodes, mapped to VTK's reference coordinates by `_to_vtk_parameters`: tensor axes `[-1, 1]`, the pyramid's base on `[-1, 1]^2` under its apex); `HCURL`/`HDIV` and pyramids' rational bases are left out with a warning; cells come from each type's cell spec (corners first), types in their specs' offset order. `dataset_to_vtk(data, cell_grid=True)` writes one back (`to_cell_grid`: `vtkDGCell.SetCellSpec`, `C1` at order 1, VTK's `A` basis above, whose nodes are Tack's equispaced nodes in Tack's order). It needs VTK's cell-type accessors on `vtkCellAttribute`, `vtkDGCell.SetCellSpec` and the evaluator's interpolate-only fix, both newer than 9.7.1: the local build in `~/Library/Modules/vtk-cell-grid`, with `TACK_VTK_DATA` for VTK's test data, which `test_vtk_cell_grid.py` reads (it skips without them; three cases are strict xfails on VTK bugs).
