    Data in pieces¶

    Large data often comes in pieces: a reader's partitions, a simulation's blocks, the share of each GPU or MPI rank. Tack keeps each piece a whole dataset and adds three things to the model: a container for the pieces, ghost marks that say which cells and points another piece owns, and global ids that name a point or cell the same way in every piece that holds it. This page explains those three. After reading it you can hold your data's pieces, mark what each owns, and know why per-piece work then adds up to the whole's answer. Running filters over pieces is the Visualization layer's Filters over pieces; moving data between ranks is the Communication layer's.

    A grid in four pieces¶

    td.split cuts a structured grid into pieces, here with one layer of each neighbour's cells around every piece. Each piece then holds more cells than it owns, but the owned cells and points of the four pieces are exactly the whole grid's.

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td
from tack.dataset import Ghosts

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

whole = td.wavelet(10)                          # 21^3 points, 8000 cells
pieces = td.split(whole, 4, ghost_layers=1)     # a PartitionedDataSet
print(pieces)

owned_cells = owned_points = 0
for piece in pieces:
    cells, points = piece.ghosts.cells.numpy(), piece.ghosts.points.numpy()
    mine = int((cells & Ghosts.SKIPPED_CELLS == 0).sum())
    owned_cells += mine
    owned_points += int((points & Ghosts.SKIPPED_POINTS == 0).sum())
    print(f"{piece.num_cells} cells held, {mine} owned")
assert (owned_cells, owned_points) == (whole.num_cells, whole.num_points)

# Global ids name a shared point the same way in both pieces that hold it.
numbered = td.generate_global_ids(pieces)
first, second = (p.global_ids.points.numpy() for p in numbered[:2])
shared = np.intersect1d(first, second)
where = {g: i for i, g in enumerate(second)}
x0, x1 = numbered[0].positions(), numbered[1].positions()
assert all(np.array_equal(x0[i], x1[where[g]]) for i, g in enumerate(first) if g in where)
print(len(shared), "points shared by the first two pieces")

    Each piece holds 2,420 cells and owns 2,000. The other 420 are its ghost layer, copies of the cells next to it that its neighbours own.

    A container of whole datasets¶

    A PartitionedDataSet is an ordered list of datasets, as VTK's vtkPartitionedDataSet is. Each piece is a complete DataSet, with its own topology and numbering, its own geometry, fields and sets, and its own device. A piece may have no cells. The container is not a dataset itself: you iterate over it (for piece in pieces), index it (pieces[i]) and take its length. td.PartitionedDataSet(list_of_datasets) makes one.

    flowchart LR
    P[PartitionedDataSet] --> A[Piece 0: a DataSet]
    P --> B[Piece 1: a DataSet]
    P --> C[Piece 2: a DataSet]
    A --- G0[owned cells + ghost layer]
    B --- G1[owned cells + ghost layer]
    P -. world .-> W[the other ranks' pieces]

    Work on one piece takes the piece. Filters, analyses and your own kernels take a DataSet, and you loop over the pieces. There is no wrapper that applies a function to every piece, because the loop already says what it does. Only algorithms whose answer needs more than one piece take the container: reductions such as statistics and integrals, probes, particle tracing, connected regions, ghost generation, and the point-cloud filters that need neighbours.

    Owners and ghosts¶

    Pieces overlap where they meet. Neighbouring pieces of a mesh share the points on their common boundary, and with ghost layers they also hold copies of each other's cells. Tack's rule is that every cell and point that several pieces hold has exactly one owner. In every other piece that holds it, its copy is a ghost, and a mark says so.

    Reductions count only what each piece owns. So the statistics of the pieces are the whole's, with every shared point counted once.

    A ghost layer is what lets work on one piece be right near its edge. A point gradient at the edge of a piece averages over every cell around the point, and the ghost layer supplies the cells across the cut. With at least one layer, the piece that owns a point holds every cell around it. Filters carry the marks to what they make, so the owned part of a filter's output, over all pieces, is the whole's output. Filters over pieces shows this and its rules.

    Ghost marks¶

    data.ghosts is a td.Ghosts, or None for data that is not in pieces. It holds one u8 per cell (ghosts.cells) and one per point (ghosts.points). Either may be None. The bits are VTK's vtkGhostType bits, so they map one to one to VTK's ghost arrays:

    Bit Value Means
    Ghosts.DUPLICATECELL 1 a copy of a cell another piece owns
    Ghosts.REFINEDCELL 8 a cell a finer AMR level covers: valid data, superseded (see AMR)
    Ghosts.HIDDENCELL 32 a cell whose data is not valid here, such as a blanked cell
    Ghosts.DUPLICATEPOINT 1 a copy of a point another piece owns
    Ghosts.HIDDENPOINT 2 a point whose data is not valid here
    Ghosts.REFINEDPOINT 8 a point where a finer AMR level has one; Tack's bit, not VTK's

    VTK's other cell bits (HIGHCONNECTIVITYCELL, LOWCONNECTIVITYCELL, EXTERIORCELL) are carried along but not read. Three masks combine the bits. Ghosts.SKIPPED_CELLS and Ghosts.SKIPPED_POINTS are what reductions leave out: duplicates, hidden and refined entities. Ghosts.MASKED_CELLS, hidden and refined cells, is what filters that make cells leave out.

    The marks are kept apart from the fields, as the geometry is, so no algorithm mistakes them for data. to_device moves them with the dataset, and the VTK interop maps them to and from vtkGhostType arrays. You can set them yourself; marks of the wrong length or type are refused.

    Hidden cells¶

    A hidden cell is one whose data is not valid, such as a blanked cell of a structured grid or a cell outside the domain. Filters never process hidden cells, and reductions leave them out. Algorithms whose output is on the same cells, such as gradients and projections, still compute there, and the marks stay on those cells.

    import numpy as np

import tack
import tack.dataset as td
from tack.dataset import Ghosts

tack.set_default_device("cpu")

data = td.uniform_grid((5, 5, 2))                          # 16 cells
data.fields["c"] = td.Field(td.Constant(data), tack.arange(16.0))

marks = np.zeros(16, np.uint8)
marks[:4] = Ghosts.HIDDENCELL                              # blank the first row
data.ghosts = td.Ghosts(cells=tack.array(marks))

summary = td.statistics(data, "c")                         # leaves the hidden cells out
assert summary.n == 12 and summary.mean == 9.5
print(summary.n, "cells counted, mean", summary.mean)

    Global ids¶

    Each piece numbers its points and cells from zero, so a shared point has a different index in each piece. Global ids name each point and cell across all the pieces: one i64 per point and per cell, the same wherever the entity is held, and unique otherwise. They are data.global_ids, a td.GlobalIds with points and cells arrays, either of which may be None.

    VTK's GlobalIds arrays come in as global ids. td.generate_global_ids makes them, as vtkGenerateGlobalIds does. Filters keep the ids of the points and cells they keep, as threshold and extract_cells do. What a filter makes new has none. Global ids let algorithms match pieces exactly rather than by position, and some algorithms, such as connected regions and the splitting of shared faces into tetrahedra, use them to decide alike in every piece.

    Where pieces come from¶

    • A structured grid is cut by td.split(data, n, ghost_layers=k). Each piece is a structured grid whose topology's offset is the grid index of its first point in the whole grid, VTK's extent start.
    • VTK gives pieces as a vtkPartitionedDataSet, which vtk_to_dataset turns into a PartitionedDataSet, ghost arrays and global ids included. A vtkPartitionedDataSetCollection becomes a dictionary of names to partitioned datasets.
    • A simulation gives you its blocks. Make a dataset of each and put them in a container (see Connect a simulation's data).

    Pieces without ghost cells can get them from td.ghost_cells(pieces), which finds the neighbours by their shared points and adds a layer, as VTK's vtkGhostCellsGenerator does. Filters over pieces covers it, along with td.remove_ghosts and td.merge_pieces, which turn pieces back into a single dataset.

    Pieces on devices and ranks¶

    A piece is a dataset, so it lives on a device of its own. One container can hold pieces on two GPUs and the CPU, and each piece's work runs where the piece is (see Data on devices).

    Over MPI, each rank holds its own pieces, and the container also holds a world, a tack.communication.World made from an mpi4py communicator (see Blocks and worlds). The algorithms that take the container then communicate with the other ranks. The per-piece loops do not change. A rank makes its container from its own pieces as td.PartitionedDataSet(mine, tc.World(MPI.COMM_WORLD)), with tc being tack.communication and MPI mpi4py's module. The MPI page shows a whole run.

    Pieces of one dataset must agree on their frame. Algorithms that compare or move positions between pieces refuse pieces on two origins, in two spatial units, or at two times (see Coordinates, units and time).

    Details¶

    Pieces without ghost cells¶

    Without ghost layers, a piece cannot tell what its neighbours make or keep from the cells, faces and edges they share. A contour point on a shared edge belongs to the owner of the edge's lower end, by position, and that piece may have no cell holding the edge. Tack does not guess. The outputs of such filters record that they lost the marks, and reductions refuse them, naming the cause. generate_global_ids of those outputs matches their points by position and decides the owners again. Filters that keep the cells and points as they are, and selections of points by their own values, need no ghost cells.

    Fields whose owners are not decided¶

    An L2 field goes by its cells' marks. A field on faces or edges, or an H1 field of order 2 or more, is refused by reductions on a dataset with marks, because who owns those values is not decided yet.
